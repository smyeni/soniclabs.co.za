In the DFT, why are samples multiplied by a counter-rotating hunting phasor with bigger and bigger phase strides for each explored bin (what's the counter-rotation actually doing or undoing)?
In FFT, why do samples have to be initially grouped into even and odd-indexed populations - enter the exponential-driven extra twiddle for odds.

I once looked at the oscilloscope signal trace. It was jumping all over the show, and fast.
So the young me was like - haybo! But what does a Fourier spectrum mean or look like if the signal is time varying like this - surely the spectrum must be time varying too.

I quietly backed off, disappointed at the feeling I was missing a point somewhere.
Enter wavelets - discovered through a research project we had two years later. They looked like time-aware Fourier spectra.

So I was like - picture a detective saying: "I'm not only interested to know who was at the crime scene (frequency components), in addition I wanna know who came and left when (when did those frequencies show up and faded)."
